Memorizing Scripture usually looks like this: you read a verse ten times, close your eyes, and discover you remember about four words of it. Then you feel guilty and stop.

PixBible is a Bible memory app built around a different idea — that memorizing a verse should feel like playing a short game, not like studying for an exam. It's out now on the App Store, and it's free to start.

Three ways to learn one verse

The core of the app is a difficulty ladder. You meet a verse three times, and each pass asks a little more of you.

1. Arrange the tiles

The verse is broken into word tiles and scrambled. You tap them back into order. This is the gentlest pass — you're not recalling the verse from nothing, you're recognizing it, which is exactly what you can actually do the first time you see it.

2. Fill in the blanks

Now the verse appears as first-letter hints — "F___ G__ s_ l____ t__ w____" — and you type it out yourself. The hints are enough to jog your memory without handing you the answer.

3. Dictation

The hardest pass. You see the verse, then you write the whole thing out from memory. No hints, no tiles. If you can clear this one, you genuinely know the verse rather than just recognizing it.

Lives and streaks, so a bad day doesn't end the habit

You start with up to 10 lives. Get a verse wrong and you lose one, but a life refills every 20 minutes — so a rough session costs you a coffee break, not your progress. Practice on consecutive days and your streak builds. Optional reminders nudge you if you'd like them.

The design goal here is unglamorous but important: make it cheap to fail. Most memorization habits die because one bad week feels like proof you can't do it.

The whole KJV, organized into courses

All 31,102 verses across the 66 books of the King James Version are in the app. Rather than dropping you into Genesis 1:1 and wishing you luck, verses are grouped into courses:

  • Beginnings — a short starter course you can try without an account
  • The Lord's Prayer
  • Warm-up — 30 topical sets (comfort, fear, thanksgiving, and so on)
  • Old Testament — all 39 books
  • New Testament — all 27 books

English practice, as a side effect

This one is worth naming because it surprised some early users. If English isn't your first language, memorizing the KJV verse by verse is a slow but real vocabulary and syntax workout. You're not drilling flashcards — you're internalizing full, well-formed sentences and then reproducing them from memory. That happens to be one of the better ways to learn a language, and here it comes attached to something you wanted to do anyway.

Why it looks like a 1990s handheld

The retro pixel styling isn't only nostalgia. Sacred text carries a weight that makes most modern app design feel either sterile or falsely cheerful. A pixel aesthetic sidesteps both — it signals "this is a game, be playful here" without dressing the Bible up in corporate gradients. It also renders beautifully on small screens, which is where you'll actually use it.

Try it in your browser first

If you'd rather not install anything yet, the web version runs the same courses at pixbible.cloud. No account needed to try the starter course.
